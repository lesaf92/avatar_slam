\section{Medium-Aware Communication}
\label{sec:comm}

\subsection{Wire format}
Each landmark part is encoded in $16 + D$ bytes: position at 5~cm resolution, isotropic horizontal and
vertical uncertainty, footprint and part height, class, medium/modality flags, observation count,
and an optional $D$-dimensional int8 descriptor; packets carry a 10-byte header and a CRC-16
(docs/spec/wire\_format\_v0.md). \todo{justify resolutions vs.\ sensor noise.}

\subsection{Link budgets}
Each node $n$ holds a token bucket per link $L$ that earns $b_L\,u_L\,s_L\,\Delta t/8$ bytes per
exchange period $\Delta t$, where $b_L$ is the raw bit rate, $u_L$ the fraction of channel time
granted to map sharing, and $s_L$ the node's time-division share (on the acoustic channel,
$1/N_L$ for $N_L$ modems). Unused tokens carry over up to $\max(4\times\text{tick}, \mathrm{MTU})$, so a node
on a 64\,bit/s modem can still send one full packet. A vehicle whose depth rules a link
out stays silent and keeps accruing tokens; a BlueROV2 that surfaces therefore spends its RF
allowance in a burst.

\subsection{Value of information per byte}
Receivers use shared landmarks to estimate the planar alignment $(x, y, \theta)$ between local
frames; the vertical offset is fixed by the waterline datum. A record at horizontal position
$\mathbf{q} = (q_x, q_y)$ with horizontal standard deviation $\sigma$ adds the Fisher information
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{q}} = \tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\,\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\!\top}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{q}},
  \qquad
  \mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{q}} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & -q_y \\ 0 & 1 & q_x \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
For every link, the sender tracks the information $\mathbf{J}_d$ it has already delivered to each
receiver domain $d$ (the domains reached directly or through the gateway). Records not yet sent
are ranked greedily by their expected D-optimal gain
\begin{equation}
  g(\mathbf{q}) = \sum_{d} \rho_d(\mathbf{q})\,
  \log\det\!\Big(\mathbf{I}_2 + \tfrac{1}{\sigma^2}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{q}}\mathbf{J}_d^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{\mathbf{q}}^{\!\top}\Big),
\end{equation}
which follows from the matrix determinant lemma. Here $\rho_d$ is the probability that a receiver in domain $d$ can match
the record. It is zero for a land-only part seen from below the surface, reduced for a
waterline-crossing part seen from the other medium (a horizontal-only coaxial match), and it
grows with the observation count. After each pick, $\mathbf{J}_d \leftarrow \mathbf{J}_d + \rho_d\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{q}}$.
All records cost the same number of bytes on a given link, so this greedy order is also the order of value per
byte. The criterion prefers well-observed parts that are spread out, which constrain yaw
at long lever arms. \todo{H2 result: bandwidth sweep (T-C5-01); at 64\,bit/s the order barely
matters, docs/LOG.md L12.}

\subsection{Gateway and surfacing}
A sensorless quay-side gateway stores and forwards: acoustic packets are relayed to RF verbatim,
and RF records are re-encoded for the acoustic link without descriptors, keeping the originator
as sender. Records of waterline-crossing classes go first, round-robin across originators.
