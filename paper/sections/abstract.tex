Teams that mix aerial, ground, surface, and underwater robots could map harbors, dams, and
offshore assets end to end, yet no SLAM system lets such a team build one consistent map
without a central server. The obstacles are physical: the water surface splits both what the
robots \emph{perceive} (optics above, acoustics below) and how they \emph{communicate}
(megabit radio links versus kilobit, intermittent acoustic links). We present \avatar{}, a
decentralized metric-semantic SLAM system for air--ground--surface--underwater teams. \avatar{}
(i)~models structures that cross the waterline as \emph{coaxial landmark parts}, turning piles,
hulls, and buoys into constraints between above-water and below-water maps;
(ii)~shares condensed, self-measured landmark maps under a medium-aware communication policy
with a compact wire format; and (iii)~aligns agents' maps with a robust, uncertainty-gated
