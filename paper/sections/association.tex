\section{Cross-Medium Association}
\label{sec:association}

\draftnote{Implemented in avatar\_py/avatar/frontend/association.py (v0.1).}

\subsection{Candidate gating}
Pairs of landmark parts are gated by medium (same medium: 3D residual; above/below: horizontal
residual only), semantic class, yaw-invariant footprint ratio, height ratio (same medium), and
descriptor cosine similarity when both carry open-vocabulary descriptors.

\subsection{Consistency graph and hypotheses}
Two candidate pairs are consistent if they use distinct landmarks and preserve horizontal
distance, and, if both are same-medium, vertical offset, within the sum of their uncertainty-scaled
gates. Hypotheses are greedy maximal cliques of this graph, in the spirit of
PCM~\cite{mangelson2018pcm}; each is refined by weighted 4-DoF Kabsch~\cite{umeyama1991} and
scored with a one-to-one MSAC score in which cross-medium inliers weigh $2/3$.

\subsection{Ambiguity and support tests}
Regular pile rows induce shifted hypotheses. We reject an alignment if a distinct competitor
reaches a fraction $\rho$ of the best score, and require more support when all inliers are
cross-medium.

\subsection{Cycle consistency over the team frame graph}
A pair with no true overlap can still produce an alignment that passes every local test
(for example, eight coaxial matches on a regular pile row). Each agent therefore checks the frame graph it knows:
its own alignments, the \texttt{FRAME\_ALIGNMENT} estimates it received, and its neighbors'
estimates of its own frame. Edges are visited in order of decreasing support (inliers, then
$\sigma$), in the manner of Kruskal's algorithm. An edge that joins two components of the accepted forest is accepted.
An edge that closes a cycle is accepted only if the composed cycle
$\Tf{a}{b}^{-1}\circ\Tf{a}{c_1}\circ\cdots\circ\Tf{c_n}{b}$ lies within a $3\sigma$ gate on
horizontal translation and yaw, where the $\sigma$ includes yaw uncertainty times the largest lever arm, with floors of
1\,m and 0.1\,rad. Stronger edges are visited first, so a rejected edge is always the weakest edge of the
inconsistent cycle it closes. A vetoed alignment is left out of the fused graph and not advertised. The check is
recomputed from scratch at every exchange, so the veto is lifted when new evidence agrees with
the edge. Edges that close no cycle cannot be checked. Table~\ref{tab:cycle} reports the
generic harbor team, where every wrong alignment links two agents with no true overlap.
