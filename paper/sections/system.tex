\section{Decentralized Backbone}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Local graph}
Agent $i$ maintains a 4-DoF factor graph~\cite{dellaert2017factor} over its poses and landmark
parts with odometry, landmark, absolute-height, and intra-agent coaxial factors:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{odo}}_{k} &= \begin{bmatrix} \Rz{\psi_{k-1}}^{\!\top}(\p_k - \p_{k-1}) - \Delta\p_k \\
  \wrap{\psi_k - \psi_{k-1} - \Delta\psi_k} \end{bmatrix}, \\
  \mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{lm}}_{k,m} &= \Rz{\psi_k}^{\!\top}(\lm_m - \p_k) - \mathbf{m}_{k,m}, \\
  \mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{z}}_{k} &= p^z_k - z^{\mathrm{meas}}_k, \qquad
  \mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{coax}}_{s} = \Pxy(\lm^{+}_s - \lm^{-}_s),
\end{align}
following the 4-DoF parameterization common in visual-inertial SLAM~\cite{qin2018vins}.

\subsection{Robustness to front-end errors}
Real front-ends confuse similar structures (a pile for its neighbor) and report
clutter. A few such associations inside the local graph are enough to break single-agent
SLAM and, through the shared records, the team. We therefore solve the local and fused graphs
with graduated non-convexity (GNC) on a truncated least-squares cost for the landmark
observations~\cite{yang2020gnc}. The optimization starts from the convex surrogate and
alternates a few Levenberg--Marquardt iterations with fixed weights and the closed-form
weight update, until every weight is 0 or 1. The inlier bound is the 0.999 quantile of the
$\chi^2_3$ distribution. Because the local graph is re-solved at every exchange, weights are
warm-started: previous weights are kept, new observations first pull the estimate, and the
surrogate then starts close to the truncated cost. \todo{result: realism study (LOG L25).}

\subsection{Heading bias}
Near steel structures the magnetometer of an underwater vehicle is unreliable, so its
heading is effectively gyro-integrated and carries a slowly varying bias. Where a platform's
heading is dead-reckoned, each agent estimates a heading bias $b$ [rad/m] with a prior from
the platform specification, and its odometry factors subtract $b$ times the distance
travelled. A reliable compass heading needs no such state.

\subsection{Condensed sharing without double counting}
Each agent periodically broadcasts its landmark parts with marginal uncertainty computed from the
\emph{local} graph only. Since shared information never contains received information, a
receiver may fuse it without the bookkeeping that DDF-SAM requires to avoid
double counting~\cite{cunningham2010ddfsam, cunningham2013ddfsam2}. \todo{formal statement +

\subsection{Fused graph}
Agent $i$ augments a copy of its local graph with a frame variable $\Tf{i}{j}$ per aligned
neighbor and robust inter-agent factors
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{r}^{\mathrm{link}} = \boldsymbol{\Pi}\,\big(\lm_a - (\mathbf{t}_{ij} + \Rz{\theta_{ij}}\,\mathbf{q}_b)\big),
  \quad \boldsymbol{\Pi} = \begin{cases} \mathbf{I}_3 & \text{same medium}\\ \Pxy & \text{cross medium}\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{q}_b$ is the neighbor's decoded landmark with covariance inflated by quantization
noise, under a Huber loss.

\subsection{Team frame}
Each agent broadcasts the frames it estimated, $\Tf{i}{j}$ with marginal uncertainty, in
\texttt{FRAME\_ALIGNMENT} messages (42~bytes each, header and CRC included). Any agent can then express a
neighbor $j$ in the frame of a designated anchor $a$ by composing the cycle-consistent estimates
it knows along the path of least accumulated $\sigma$. Frames are 4-DoF, so the composition is
exact and costs no additional solve. Agents that never exchange landmarks directly (e.g., the UAV and a
BlueROV2) meet through this chain.
